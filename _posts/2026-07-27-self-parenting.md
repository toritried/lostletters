Lately, I've been feeling particularly inadequate when it comes to taking care of myself because I've been slacking on a couple salubrious behaviors: eating more fiber and exercising more regularly. Inspired by <a target="_blank" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O9N4NknlqUs">this Muchelleb YouTube video</a>, I want to take a page from parenting resources to figure out how to better care for myself.<!--excerpt-->

I feel like a disenfranchised self-parent, one that has given up on trying to modify or correct my behavior because any effort I've tried to do so in the past has failed to produce lasting, long-term results. From what I gathered online, if you feel like a failing parent, you can try resetting your expectations (perhaps my expectations of perfection on these fronts aren't appropriate and should be more modest), giving yourself grace (maybe I'm just going through a tougher time than usual and it's not a reflection of myself worth), and creating a list of intentions instead of expectations that serves as a guide instead of rules (this is what I'm going to outline below).

In the video I linked above, there are seven tips for self-parenting, but I only want to focus the ones that I can use to create a playbook for the issues I'm currently struggling with:

<b>Collaboration</b>: In order to collaboratively problem solve with myself, I need to first be on my side instead of approaching myself with adversarial frustration. The video suggests a three-step process for self-collaboration:
1. <b>Fostering self-empathy</b>; ex: explore how a demand to eat more fiber or exercise from my managing self to my doing self makes me feel
2. <b>Investigating why this behavior is important</b>; ex: these behaviors make me feel my best and promote longevity, which is important to me because I want to be around as long as possible for my partner
3. <b>Defining what a realistic plan looks like</b>  

<b>Giving myself choices</b>: For food, that could look like coming up with a few fiber-forward recipe options that avoid the potential cyclospora targets, and deciding what sounds the tastiest. For exercise, that could look like giving myself the option to take a short peloton class with fun music or just cycling to a pre-programmed workout without sound so I could listen to an audiobook. This kind of reminds me of coming up with a dopamine menu, so I might try to outline one for myself for the sake of creatively exploring all my options. 

<b>Taking myself outside if I get "weird"</b>: If I can't make up my mind or get overwhelmed and paralyzed by choices, I will take myself outside for a bit to get out of my head. Adults need outdoor time to reset much like children.

As a means of evaluating these strategies, I'll try to be more mindful this week about noticing my feelings and recording them in a journal when possible, then work through the rest of the intentions I've outlined above to hopefully create some modest behavior change. Since I'm really new to this and have no other parenting experience (I have no interest in actual childrearing), I'm curious if others have found success with self-parenting strategies.
