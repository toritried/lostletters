It's been over a month since my wife came down with a debilitating disease called Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (SIBO). She's been basically bed ridden, full of discomfort (antibiotics helped a lot) and fatigue this entire time with a few exceptions, so I've been in full caretaker mode, shopping for and cooking vegan, low FODMAP recipes for her for weeks now. All that to say, I haven't had time or energy for posting here. However, there have been some personal highlights from this past month that I wanted to share.<!--excerpt-->

With health being at the forefront of my mind, I've never been more grateful for insurance. To take full advantage of it, I got my first eye exam in several years and subsequently bought glasses for the second time in my life. I'm particularly excited about my new pair because I finally got the new ruby-colored Transition lenses, which look super cute and transition a lot faster than the previous generation of transition lenses. All told, my glasses were a little over $100 USD from <a target="_blank" href="http://rwrd.io/xcsg4z8?c">Zenni</a>, which feels like a bargain compared to the hundreds of dollars I could have spent at an eyeglasses boutique. They really help my vision immensely, which is a good reminder to get your eyes checked more than once a a decade.

<img style="margin: auto; display: block;" src="/graphics/blog/2026/ruby-lenses.png" alt="four lenses demonstrating the transition gradient from clear to ruby" title="ruby transition lenses">

I've also been trying to take my own health more seriously while we're insured, so I'm starting to work with an awesome, <a target="_blank" href="https://asdah.org/haes/">HAES-practicing</a> dietician to work through some digestive, physical, and psychological issues I've been putting off for a while. I'm hoping learn how to bolster the nutrition of my meals and address some cognitive distortions like my scarcity mindset around finding vegan options on the go or when traveling. There's a lot to unpack, but I'm grateful I really vibe with my dietician's approach that's part therapy, part education. 

Speaking of education, I'm a student again! Not in a formal university sense, but I am currently taking Spanish classes with <a target="_blank" href="https://learn.lingoda.com/en/referral/u7ewch">Lingoda</a>, and will be joining their Spanish language sprint starting mid-October. During the sprint, I'll be taking classes every other day for the whole two-month period and will hopefully advance past previous levels of proficiency. I'm determined to earn half my money back by successfully completing the language sprint by ensuring I take a class every other day without missing any. Although it seems intimidating, I'm not doing it alone. I'll be taking classes at the same Spanish level as my wife's best friend, who is also taking the sprint at the same time as me and has been a blessing of a co-caretaker during this ordeal.

My wife, her best friend, and I, recently went out on a night when my wife was feeling a bit better than usual due to just having finished her antibiotics course of treatment. We got dinner and drinks, then opened some Magic: The Gathering card packs. The friend taught us an unofficial, "kitchen table" style of Magic where any card from your hand can be placed down as a wild Land card to provide mana for any of your other cards. We only had to buy three packs to play like this, and we had such a fun time getting familiar with the new cards. If my wife weren't so sick, I'd consider hosting a M:tG event for my birthday where I'd hire a magician to perform magic for us as well, but that's just not in the cards this year, but we'll probably just run back the drinks, pack openings, and play at home instead.

On the crafty side of things, my best friend and I wanted to take advantage of the fall weather and knit outside. Despite the cooler temperature, we still got eaten up by mosquitoes. However, it did give me the motivation to finish a <a target="_blank" href="https://www.ravelry.com/patterns/library/catkin">Catkin capelet</a> that I started in October of last year! I don't have good finished product photos, but I do have this one image of it being blocked, which shows off the whole garment as it's held in place with T-pins on the blocking mats.

<img style="margin: auto; display: block;" src="/graphics/blog/2026/catkin.jpg" alt="a mostly square-shaped brown, orange, and pink capelet laid flat on blocking mats, pinned in place while it dries" title="Catkin capelet">

Riding high on the finished project energy of the Catkin, I went on a finishing spree and made this baby set for my friends' upcoming child in all of three days, then knit another hat and seven pairs of socks in five more days. The soon-to-be parents were so shocked and delighted by the gifts that it just made me want to knit more for them. They also already care for wool garments with wool wash, so I know they'll take good care of the items even though I used superwash yarn to make the gifts easier to take care of as new parents. I can't wait to see the baby in this cute little set, and the mom wanted to get clothes in her size to match, which I found totally flattering and adorable.

Lastly, I was extremely lucky and won the free grand prize raffle at my local Pelikan hub, which was a <a target="_blank" href="https://yosekastationery.com/products/pelikan-m200-fountain-pen-classic-cherry-blossom-special-edition">$232 gold-nibbed Pelikan M200 Cherry Blossom fountain pen</a>. I never win anything, so it was a huge surprise that I won anything let alone the best prize. It was my first time ever attending a Pelikan hub, and I didn't have any of the brand's pens or inks, so I was really delighted to walk away with this pen. Furthermore, it's my first ever bold nib (I almost exclusively buy Medium Fine nibs because it fits best for my handwriting in the small Hobonichi Weeks grid), and it really shows off all my favorite glittery inks, which I thoroughly enjoy.
